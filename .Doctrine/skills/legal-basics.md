# Skill: Legal Basics

**Scope.** Legal hygiene, not legal practice — contracts read, obligations
tracked, filings calendared, escalations flagged. This is not legal advice.

## Principles
- Nothing in this skill is legal advice, and no output pretends to be;
  serious questions go to human counsel, framed with the facts prepared.
- Read the whole document before summarizing any of it. The clause that
  bites lives in the section the summary skipped.
- Obligations are only real when registered: who owes what, by when,
  evidenced where. Memory is not a compliance system.
- Drafts come from established patterns; an invented clause is how a
  template becomes a lawsuit.
- Unusual or asymmetric terms are flagged, never negotiated solo.

## Defaults
- Obligations register per executed agreement: party, duty, deadline,
  evidence location, owner. Reviewed on a standing cadence.
- Escalation list for human counsel: any term that is unusual,
  asymmetric, uncapped, or plain unclear — quoted verbatim, with section
  references and a plain-language note on why it's flagged.
- Filing calendar: every registration, renewal, and report with an owner
  and a date; an empty owner field blocks the entry.
- Contract drafts assemble from the pattern library actually used before;
  deviations highlighted for counsel, never silently included.
- Signatures, negotiations, and commitments are the human's alone
  (.Doctrine.md §5); the skill prepares, flags, and tracks.

## Do
- Summarize with section citations so the human can verify any line
  against the source in seconds.
- Keep the privacy policy matched to the ACTUAL data map
  (`skills/privacy-compliance.md`); a policy describing aspirations is a
  liability, not a shield.
- Diff every counterparty redline against the last agreed version and
  list the changes plainly before anyone reads a summary.
- Record what was actually signed — final executed copy, dated, filed —
  not the draft that almost was.

## Don't
- Don't summarize a document you haven't fully read.
- Don't invent clause language; adapt from patterns or escalate.
- Don't let "standard terms" pass unread because they're standard.
- Don't negotiate, accept, or reject terms — flag them.
- Don't state legal conclusions; state facts, quote terms, list options.

## Review checklist
- [ ] Whole document read before any summary or extraction
- [ ] Obligations registered: who, what, when, evidence, owner
- [ ] Unusual/asymmetric terms on the counsel escalation list, verbatim
- [ ] Filing calendar entries each carry owner and date
- [ ] Privacy policy reconciled against the actual data map
- [ ] Advice disclaimer present; nothing signed or negotiated solo

## Common failure modes
The skimmed contract whose section 14 was the whole point; obligations
tracked in someone's head until they leave; a privacy policy describing
the product the team wishes it had; boilerplate trusted because it is
boilerplate; an "almost final" draft filed as if executed; flagged terms
that quietly became accepted because nobody owned the follow-up.
